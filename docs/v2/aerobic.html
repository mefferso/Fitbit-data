Eligibility and weather

Local Tempest data are accepted as representative of nearby runs. Weather is displayed and used to make like-for-like comparisons; no unvalidated precise dewpoint or solar correction is applied to the oxygen equation. Slope, HR sensor noise, pacing changes, and intentional finishes may still matter.

Segment rule: 5-minute overlapping windows after minute 10, ending before the last 2 minutes; at least 80% GPS coverage; minute pace variation ≤10%; HR range ≤12 bpm; pace 8:00–16:00/mi. At least four separate eligible runs and a meaningful HR spread are required for a provisional model.

Calculation rules, sensitivity and limitations

Intervals and fast finishes can contaminate simple regressions. We extract only smooth, quality-controlled windows and fit a robust line at the run level, so long runs do not overwhelm shorter runs. Windows overlap; they are not independent statistical observations.

For estimation, each run contributes its median eligible segment (and run-to-run stability is checked). The display additionally shows HRmax sensitivity at ±10 bpm. If HR variation or fit quality is poor, no absolute estimate is reported.

Matching the same heart-rate band controls one major variable but not terrain, surface, training fatigue, medication, weather, or individual changes in running economy. Weekly chart points should be treated as descriptive, not clinical precision.

Not a validated clinical or coaching-grade VO₂max test. Validation requires a lab gas-exchange test and preferably standardized submaximal comparisons. VDOT from a real race is a useful performance cross-check but not independent proof of maximal oxygen consumption.

Sources: published Fitbit/Google Health TCX heart-rate and distance records in this repository; Tempest station readings; ACSM running oxygen-cost equation; Daniels–Gilbert VDOT formula. No secrets or location coordinates are needed.
